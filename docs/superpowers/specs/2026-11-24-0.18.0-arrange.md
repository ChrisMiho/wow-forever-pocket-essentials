# 0.18.0: arrange it yourself

Part 3 of the away batch (`2026-11-24-away-batch.md`; its "Rules for every part" apply).
Written before the code, by the builder, with nobody to ask. **This is a first version for
the owner to react to: there was no brainstorm.** Every choice the charter left open is
marked **Choice**; they are the builder's, not the owner's. Blizzard's source is
`~/.cache/wow-ui-source/forever` at `c01a87b`, build 1.60.1 (71091); paths below are under
`Interface/AddOns/`. `E/` is `Blizzard_EditMode/`, `G/` is `Blizzard_GamepadActionBars/`,
`DOC/` is `Blizzard_APIDocumentationGenerated/`.

Nothing here is tested on a device. The offline tests prove the logic only.

The owner: "I love the idea of me tweaking things where I want it, my only concern is it
not being perfectly symmetrical." So the tool is small, and symmetry is what it guards
hardest: twins move as mirrors, steps are whole, the centre line catches a piece that comes
near it, and a check says out loud when a pair is not an exact mirror.

## What is built

- A switch **Arrange it yourself** (Controller tab, Off by default), and a second one,
  **Arrange: spell wheels** (Off by default), for the one piece the charter asked a
  question about.
- A row **Arrange the screen** with a button **Arrange** that opens a small window, the
  Arrange screen, in the manner of "Move on screen" (`Move.lua`).
- A new file, `addons/PocketEssentials/Arrange.lua` (`PE.Arrange`), in the TOC after
  `SlimBars.lua`. `ControllerLayout.lua` holds the list of pieces and applies the offsets
  inside the plan, so the overlap check, the snapshot, the spell pictures and the slim bars
  all see the moved spots.

## The pieces

A piece is what the owner picks and moves; it may be several rectangles of the plan that
belong together (the map and its place-name strip). "Moves live" means it moves as the
arrow is pressed. "On Apply" means the game places it from its Edit Mode layout: the move is
collected, the layout is written once when **Apply** is tapped, and one reload shows it.

**Choices:**
- **Groups, not single rectangles.** The pet bar stays stacked on the player bar, the cast
  bar under the target bar, the three timers in their stack. Fewer pieces, and no way to
  pull a stack apart by accident. A later version can split them.
- **Not in the list:** the PE button ("Move on screen" already moves it, and stays the way
  to do so); the game's hint box (the game anchors it top left itself,
  `Blizzard_Gamepad/UI/PersistentInputLegend/GamepadPersistentInputLegend.xml:40-42`, and
  it is not an Edit Mode piece); the target of target on its own (the game hangs it on the
  target frame, `Blizzard_UnitFrame/Mainline/TargetFrame.lua:732-737`; it follows the
  target).
- **The black map border** is ours but is pinned to the round map's centre
  (`ControllerLayout.lua`, `PlaceDisc`), so it goes with the map after the reload and is not
  a piece of its own.

### Why some move live and some do not

- Every Edit Mode system frame has `SetScale`, `SetPoint`, `ClearAllPoints`, `SetShown` and
  `Hide` replaced by Lua of the game's (`E/Shared/EditModeSystemTemplates.lua:9-17, 32-36`).
  Calling one from the addon is a call into Blizzard's Lua that writes fields (charter rule
  2). So the map (`MinimapCluster`), chat, quest list, buffs, debuffs, XP bars, tooltip
  spot, swing timers and unit frames are never touched as frames. Their spot is a number in
  our Edit Mode layout (`anchorInfo.offsetX`, `offsetY`:
  `DOC/EditModeManagerDocumentation.lua:167-168`), written with `C_EditMode.SaveLayouts`
  (`:110`) through the path the option rows already use (`RewritePieces` and `RunRewrites`
  in `ControllerLayout.lua`), and the game places the frame from it
  (`EditModeSystemTemplates.lua:373-375`) at the reload.
- The live pieces are ours (the spell panels, the slim bars, the tap guards), or game
  regions the layout already places with device-proven engine calls in `PlaceNow`: the bar
  anchors under `GamepadMainActionBarFrame.PageUnit` (plain frames,
  `G/ActionBarTemplates.xml:659-663`, not Edit Mode systems) and the two shoulder pictures
  (textures, `G/MainActionBarFrame.xml:18-26`). A live move calls nothing new: the same
  `PlaceNow`, with other numbers.

## The spell wheel (the charter's question)

**It can be moved safely, as a pair, up and down. It is built, behind its own switch.**

- The wheel is the flyout of a sub-menu button. Each time it opens the game pins it on the
  button's own centre: `GamepadFlyoutMixin:AttachToButton`, `G/FlyoutRadial.lua:218-220`
  (`self:SetPoint("CENTER", UIParent, "BOTTOMLEFT", button:GetCenter())`).
- The two buttons (`LeftClassAction`, `RightClassAction`) and their prompts are anchored to
  `PageUnit.BottomCenteredAnchor` at -138 / +138, -50 and -80
  (`G/MainActionBarFrame.xml:94-135`); the game re-anchors a button to that same frame from
  the spot it read at load (`G/ClassActionFlyout.lua:24-25, 50-54`,
  `G/ActionBarStyles.lua:128-130`).
- `BottomCenteredAnchor` is one of the five frames the layout already places
  (`ControllerLayout.lua`, `BAR_FRAMES`, spot `bottom`, at 0, 238). Moving it moves both
  buttons, both prompts and where both wheels open, with the calls that have run on the
  device since 0.12.0. The LT+RT bar is placed on its own and does not follow.
- **What cannot be done within the rules:** moving one wheel without the other, or the two
  apart or together. That needs a new anchor on the buttons themselves, which the game
  overwrites from `self.anchorInfo` (a field we may not write) whenever the button's look
  changes. Left out.
- So the piece moves **up and down only**: one anchor carries both sides, so a sideways move
  would push both the same way and break the symmetry. Left and Right are refused for it.
- Raising it 30 or so clears the swing timers and the map's edge of the screenshots; the
  overlap warning shows what it reaches instead (the wheel itself is drawn only while
  open, so the warning counts the buttons and prompts, not the wheel's circle).

## How a move is stored

An offset from the built-in spot, in UI units (the plan's unit: 1365.33 by 768; one screen
pixel at 720p is 1.07 units), x to the right, y up, whole numbers. The built-in layout
stays underneath: a later version that moves a built-in spot moves the owner's piece with
it, by the same offset.

- **Live pieces: per character**, in this character's own saved file,
  `PE.charDB.ctrlArrange = { offsets = { [key] = { x =, y = } }, alone = { [pair] = true } }`
  (`alone`: the pairs whose "Twins" is Free).
- **On-Apply pieces: with the layout's options**, `opts.offsets = { [key] = { x =, y = } }`,
  and `opts.offsetsWritten`, the offsets the Edit Mode layout holds now (what the last
  successful write put in it). A piece whose two differ is "waiting for Apply".
- The two switches: `opts.arrange`, `opts.arrangeWheel`, 0 or 1, with the other rows.

**Choice, and a departure from the charter's "per character":** the layout's options
(`PE.db.options.ctrlLayout`) are saved for the whole account, as every row of the Controller
tab is, 0.17.0's "Slim bars" included; and the "Pocket Essentials" Edit Mode layout the
on-Apply pieces are written into is one layout for the whole account
(`layoutType = Account`). An on-Apply offset kept per character would disagree with the one
shared layout on every other character until that character wrote it again and reloaded. So
an offset is stored where the spot it changes is stored: on-Apply pieces for the account,
live pieces per character. "Per-character feature switches" is listed in the charter as not
in this batch; when it comes, these go with it.

**While the switch is Off, saved offsets do not apply.** Off is the built-in arrangement,
exactly as 0.17.0 places it; the offsets stay in the saved files, untouched, for when it is
switched On again.

Bad or old saved values are ignored without an error: an entry that is not a table, an x or
y that is not a number (or not a finite one, or beyond 1,500 either way), a key that names
no piece of this version. An ignored value is not deleted (a later version may know the
key); "Reset all" removes everything.

## Inside the plan

`Plan.Moved(v, key)` gives a piece's offset: from `v` when `v` is an options table that has
an `arrange` value (the option rows and the tests), else from `Plan.session`, which the
layout sets once per session when the switch was On at the first placement, and never when
it was Off. So with the switch Off every function of the plan returns what 0.17.0 returned.

The offsets are added inside `Plan.FaceSpots`, `Plan.BarSpots` (the real bars follow their
inner panel; the bottom anchor follows `spell wheels`), `Plan.RealBarRects`,
`Plan.Shoulders` (new; `Plan.SHOULDERS` stays the built-in numbers), `Plan.SlimRects`,
`Plan.Wheels`, `Plan.Pieces` (each Edit Mode piece carries its key and the offset it was
built with) and `Plan.Rects`. `Plan.Overlaps(rects)` is the overlap check the tests had,
moved into the plan: every pair of rectangles that overlap, with the size of the overlap,
the game's own button-and-prompt pair left out as before.

`FaceBars.lua` and `SlimBars.lua` already place from these functions, so a session that
starts with the switch On places everything at the moved spots with no extra code. For a
live move `FaceBars.lua` gets one new function, "Place" (reads the spots again and places
its panels and tap guards), and `Arrange.lua` places the slim bars' own frames again from
`Plan.SlimRects` (through `PE.SlimBars.view`; `SlimBars.lua` is not changed).

The tap guards under the inner panels are cut at the player frames' edge and under the
targeting pictures (`FaceBars.lua`, `BackingRects`). The cuts move with the panel, so a
moved panel keeps a guard of the same shape: a tap on a picture must never reach the
invisible real button under it.

## Symmetry

- **Twins move as mirrors.** With "Twins: mirrored" (the default for every pair) a step on
  one piece moves it and then sets its twin to the exact mirror about the screen's centre
  line: the same height, the opposite side. The built-in spots of every pair are exact
  mirrors (a test holds that), so equal and opposite offsets are an exact mirror.
- **Per pair, "Twins: free"** moves only the picked piece. Switching back to mirrored puts
  the twin on the mirror of the picked piece at once, and says so in the log.
- **Steps** are whole units: **small 2** (about two screen pixels) and **big 10**. An offset
  is always a whole number, so a mirror is exact, never off by a fraction. **Choice:** 2 and
  10; one tap is one step, no hold-to-repeat (Move on screen has it; here the big step does
  that job and nothing has to tick).
- **Centre snap, 6 units.** For a piece with no twin: a sideways step that crosses the
  centre line, or moves toward it and ends within 6 of it, lands the piece's middle exactly
  on it. A step away from the line is never caught, so every spot can still be reached. The
  middle is that of the piece's first rectangle (the round map for Map). The window says
  "On the centre line" when it is. **Choice:** 6 units; "toward only".
- **The mirror check.** Every pair is compared: exact mirror or not. The window shows the
  result for the picked piece's pair and counts the others ("1 other pair is not
  mirrored"); the log gets a line per pair on Apply, on each change of a pair's setting and
  in the snapshot.

## The Arrange screen

Opened from the Controller tab (**Arrange**). A window of the addon's own, built the first
time it opens, in the top left corner; it moves to another corner when the picked piece's
outline is under it. The settings window goes away while it is open and comes back when it
closes, as with Move on screen.

- The title names the picked piece. A line under the buttons says "Moves as you press" or
  "Moves after Apply and a reload".
- **Previous piece / Next piece** walk the list above.
- **Up, Down, Left, Right**: one step each.
- **Reset** (in the middle of the arrows): this piece back to its built-in spot (and its
  twin with it while the pair is mirrored).
- **Step: small / Step: big**: one button, tap to change.
- **Twins: mirrored / Twins: free**: one button; greyed "No twin" for a piece without one.
- **Apply**: see below. **Reset all**: asks once ("Tap again to reset all"), then clears
  every offset.
- Four lines of text: how far the piece is from its built-in spot and whether it is on the
  centre line; the mirror check; the overlap warning; which pieces wait for Apply.
- On the screen itself: a yellow outline with the piece's name over each rectangle of the
  picked piece, a thinner yellow one over its twin, and a red one over every piece it
  overlaps. For an on-Apply piece the yellow outline is where it **will** be; the piece
  itself stays where it is until the reload. The outlines are the plan's rectangles (what
  the overlap check uses), not a measurement of the real frame: the quest list is taken at
  its least height, the tooltip at 200 by 100.

**Touch and controller.** Every control is a button of the addon's (`UI:CreateButton`):
a tap presses it. With the controller the window uses the navigation every window of the
addon uses (`UI:AttachNav`: the d-pad moves the highlight, A presses, B closes; the keys
are taken only out of a fight and given back when it closes or a fight starts). The d-pad
therefore walks to an arrow button and A presses it, as in Move on screen. **No new way of
reading the controller, no chat box opened or focused, nothing else of the game called.**

**Refused steps** (the piece stays, the window says why, one log line):
- it would put a rectangle of the piece (or of its mirrored twin) off the screen, unless it
  brings back one that was already off;
- Left or Right on the spell wheels;
- the layout is not placed (switched off, or another Edit Mode layout is active).

**A fight.** The window cannot be opened in a fight. A fight starting closes it. Every step
is saved as it is made, so nothing is lost; pieces waiting for Apply go on waiting, and the
window says so when it is opened again. Nothing of the game's is moved in a fight: a live
placement goes through `PE:RunOutOfCombat`.

## Apply, Reset, Reset all

- **Apply** writes every on-Apply piece whose offset differs from what the layout holds
  into our Edit Mode layout, in one `SaveLayouts` call, and asks for one reload (the
  settings window comes back with "Reload now"). With nothing waiting it writes nothing and
  asks for nothing, and says the arrangement is saved. Refused while Edit Mode is open. If
  the game refuses the write, the pieces go on waiting and the Errors page has the reason.
- Live pieces need no Apply: they are saved and placed at each step.
- **Reset** and **Reset all** change offsets; a live piece is back at once, an on-Apply
  piece waits for Apply like any other move.
- Any other row that rewrites a piece (Spell panel size, Slim bars, Start over) writes it
  with its offset, and the layout's record of what it holds is kept right.

## The overlap warning

After every step the plan's rectangles (with the offsets, at this session's Spell panel
size, Slim bars, Controls reminder and Map buttons) go through `Plan.Overlaps`. Every pair
that includes a rectangle of the picked piece is named in the window ("Overlaps: Chat") and
outlined in red. It warns and never refuses. The total number of overlapping pairs on the
whole screen is in the window too, so an overlap made earlier is not forgotten.

**Choice:** the two rows kept for the future buff boxes ("room") are moved with the slim
bars but do not count in the warning: nothing is drawn there yet.

What it cannot know: the spell panels of each trigger state are all in the plan, but a
frame's real size is the game's (see "outlines" above). The snapshot, taken after the
reload, measures the real frames.

## The switches

- **Arrange it yourself**, Off by default. **Counts from the next reload**, like Slim bars,
  and the reload is asked for at once: on-Apply pieces only change with a rewrite and a
  reload, so a change of the switch rewrites the pieces whose offset in the layout differs
  from what the new value asks for (On: the saved offsets; Off: none), out of a fight,
  refused while Edit Mode is open. The value the session started with decides whether
  `Arrange.lua` is ever called and whether the plan adds any offset.
- Off means: `Arrange.lua` defines its functions and does nothing else: no frame, no event,
  no hook, no game call, no snapshot source. `Plan.session` is never set, so every spot is
  0.17.0's. The **Arrange** button says to switch it on and reload.
- **Arrange: spell wheels**, Off by default, counts at once (the piece is live): Off, the
  piece is not in the list and its saved offset is not applied.
- The Controller layout going off, or another Edit Mode layout being picked, puts the live
  pieces back with everything else (`RestoreNow`, unchanged) and closes the window.

## Logging

With the Debug log on, key `ctrlLayout`, each line starting "arrange:".

- when the session starts with the switch On: one line with both switches, one line per
  saved offset with the piece's resulting spot, one line per ignored saved value and why,
  the mirror check, and the pieces waiting for Apply;
- the switch row tapped: the new value and that it counts from the next reload;
- the window opened and closed, with why;
- each step: the piece, the step, its new offset, "snapped to the centre line" when it was,
  and its twin's new offset when mirrored; each refused step and why; a live placement held
  for after a fight;
- each Reset and Reset all, with the offsets they removed;
- each change of a pair's Twins setting;
- **on Apply:** every piece's offset and resulting rectangle (left, bottom, width, height),
  moved or not, so the arrangement can become the built-in one; every overlapping pair with
  the overlap's size; the mirror check per pair; which pieces are written; and the game's
  answer.

One line per tap, never per frame. **Save a snapshot** lists the offsets, the pieces
waiting for Apply and the mirror check (`PE.ControllerLayout:AddSnapshotSource`), next to
the real frames' measured places.

## Tests (written first)

`tests/features/arrange_test.lua`, and a block in `layout_test.lua` for the plan.

- the plan: each offset moves exactly its piece's rectangles and no other, at all five Spell
  panel sizes and in each trigger state; the real bars and the wheels follow; the Edit Mode
  pieces' anchors carry the offset; the built-in twins are exact mirrors;
- Off ignores saved offsets (plan, pieces, placement), and `Arrange.lua` is never called;
- bad and stale saved values are ignored without error;
- steps, small and big; the mirrored twin; Twins free and back; the centre snap from both
  sides and stepping away from it; Reset; Reset all with its second tap;
- a refused step at the screen's edge and sideways on the wheels;
- a live piece moves at once with no layout write and no reload; the bars, the targeting
  pictures, the spell panels with their tap guards and the slim bars are placed at the new
  numbers;
- Apply writes the waiting pieces in one write and asks for one reload; with nothing
  waiting it writes nothing; refused with Edit Mode open; a failed write leaves them
  waiting;
- the switch: Off by default, a change rewrites what differs and asks for the reload;
- a fight: the window does not open, closes when one starts, and nothing of the game's is
  called;
- the mirror check, the overlap warning, the log lines of Apply, the snapshot's lines;
- touch and the controller both press the buttons.

Tests prove the logic. Only the device can show:

- that a live move really moves the pieces, and that moving the bar anchors again and again
  out of a fight brings no "action blocked" (the calls are the ones `PlaceNow` makes at
  every login and layout event);
- that the game takes the rewritten layout and puts each piece at its offset after the
  reload (the path the Spell panel size row has used since 0.15.0);
- that the wheels open where the moved buttons are;
- that the window's size suits the 720p screen, and that the d-pad reaches every button;
- that 2 and 10 are the right steps and 6 the right snap.

Each has a row in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`. The plainest fallback is the switch: Off and a
reload, and everything is where 0.17.0 puts it.

## The builder's decisions, in one list

1. Groups of rectangles as pieces; the list above; the PE button left to Move on screen.
2. On-Apply offsets for the account (with the one account-wide layout), live offsets per
   character.
3. Steps 2 and 10 units, one step per tap, no hold-to-repeat.
4. Centre snap 6 units, toward the line only, for pieces without a twin.
5. Mirroring means "the twin is set to the mirror of the piece you move"; switching back
   from free mirrors the twin onto the picked piece.
6. The spell wheels: movable up and down only, as a pair, behind their own switch.
7. "Arrange it yourself" counts from the next reload; saved offsets do not apply while Off.
8. Outlines and the overlap warning come from the plan's rectangles, not from measuring
   frames.
9. The rooms kept for the buff boxes do not count as overlaps.
10. Apply closes the window so the reload request is in view; Reset all asks twice.

## As built

- **Files.** `addons/PocketEssentials/Arrange.lua` (new, in the TOC after `SlimBars.lua`).
  `ControllerLayout.lua`: `Plan.ARRANGE`, `ARRANGE_BY`, `ARRANGE_LIMIT`, `Plan.session`,
  `Plan.Moved`, `Plan.Shoulders`, `Plan.Overlaps`, `Plan.SCREEN`, `Plan.Pending`,
  `Plan.MarkWritten`; the offsets inside `BarSpots`, `FaceSpots`, `SlimRects`, `Rects`,
  `Wheels` and `Pieces`; the three rows; and on the `Snap` table (the file has no room for
  a local) `View`, `ArrangeCall`, `ArrangeFilter`, and `mod:View`, `ArrangeReady`,
  `ArrangePlace`, `ArrangeApply`. `FaceBars.lua`: the new "Place" and the tap guards' cuts
  moving with their panel. `SlimBars.lua` is not changed.
- **Tests.** All in `tests/features/arrange_test.lua` (35 tests), the plan's too, not in a
  block of `layout_test.lua` as planned above; `layout_test.lua` only got the three new
  rows in its list of rows.
- **The session's value** is the switch as the login found it (`OnLoad`), not as the first
  placement found it (Slim bars' way): a tap on the row before the layout was first placed
  cannot start a session half way.
- **`Plan.Moved` and a table without `arrange`.** An options table with no `arrange` value
  at all means "the session's", like a number or nil. The saved options always have the
  value, so the option rows decide by the row. A test that wants the built-in numbers in a
  session that is On passes `arrange = 0`.
- **A live step places the whole layout again** (`mod:ArrangePlace` is `SyncBars`: the
  hint box, the chat's box, the map's border, the bars, the targeting pictures, the spell
  pictures' start), then `Arrange.lua`'s "Placed" places our panels and slim bars. More
  than a step needs, but nothing that does not already run at every login and layout
  event. One "bars placed" line per step in the log.
- **The centre snap may leave an offset that is not whole** for a piece whose built-in
  middle is not on a whole unit (the chat, the quest list); the middle pieces (map, timers,
  XP bars) are on whole units. A twin is never snapped, so mirrors stay whole.
- **`opts.offsetsWritten` is only made once a moved piece is written,** so a player who
  never arranges anything keeps the saved options 0.17.0 wrote.
- **The window** is about 620 by 480 pixels at the medium text size on the 720p screen:
  four rows of buttons and five lines of small text. It picks, of the four corners, the one
  that covers least of the picked piece and its twin (top left first).
- **Reset all** removes every saved offset, the ignored ones too, and leaves the pairs'
  Twins settings as they are.
- **The taint check:** ten new lines, all `ours` or `engine` (strings and the addon's own
  functions). No call into Blizzard's Lua was added and none that writes.
- **The API audit** found no new game name: nothing in `tests/api/` changed.
- **Not done:** hold-to-repeat; single rectangles as pieces; the PE button and the hint box
  as pieces; one spell wheel on its own; a preview of an Edit Mode piece other than its
  outline; offsets per character for the Edit Mode pieces.
