# 0.17.0: the slim bars

Part 2 of the away batch (`2026-11-24-away-batch.md`; its "Rules for every part" apply).
The approved design is `2026-11-24-playing-screen-layout-design.md`, "0.17.0: the slim
bars". Written before the code, by the builder, with nobody to ask: every choice the
design left open is marked **Choice**. Blizzard's source is
`~/.cache/wow-ui-source/forever` at `c01a87b`, build 1.60.1 (71091); paths below are under
`Interface/AddOns/`. `U/` is `Blizzard_UnitFrame/`, `E/` is `Blizzard_EditMode/`, `DOC/` is
`Blizzard_APIDocumentationGenerated/`.

Nothing here is tested on a device. The offline tests prove the logic only.

## What is built, and what is not

Built: five plain bars of our own (player, pet, target, target of target, the target's
cast bar) in a new file, `addons/PocketEssentials/SlimBars.lua` (`PE.SlimBars`); the
game's four unit frames made invisible while the bars show; the tooltip's spot in the
bottom right corner; the switch **Slim bars**, Off by default.

**Not built: the buff and debuff boxes** (the pet's and the target's). They wait for the
probe's "Buffs in a fight" run. Room is kept for both rows, and the overlap check guards
that room, so they can be added without moving a bar. Until then, with Slim bars On, **the
target's buffs and debuffs are not shown anywhere**: the game draws them on the target
frame, which is invisible. The CHANGELOG and the test steps say so.

Also gone from the screen while Slim bars is On, because the game draws them on the
invisible frames and the design asks for none of them: the portraits, the level circle
and elite or rare border of the target, the raid mark and the threat number on the
target, the resting, fighting and group-leader marks on the player frame, the pet's
happiness and attack flash, and anything a class adds under the player frame (totems,
`U/Mainline/TotemFrame.xml:56`; class bars, `U/Mainline/ClassPowerBar.xml:4`, are
children of the player frame). The combo point frame on this client has no parent
(`U/Camelot/ComboFrame.xml:3`) and keeps its own see-through: what it does over an
invisible player frame is for the device.

21.3, taken as **21**.

**Choice: the bars sit 115 further out than the design's table.** The table puts the
player bar "above the left diamonds, spanning both", which is x 103 to 383. The mockup had
one piece beside the map there; the game has three (the prompt, the sub-menu button and
the targeting picture, design spec, table row "The green and red targeting pictures"), and
they fill x 102 to 214 at exactly the bar's height. The rule of the design ("keeps the
arrangement, moves the piece the least") leaves three ways: out past the targeting
picture (115 sideways, nothing else changes), up over the sub-menu button (70 up, but then
the pet bar and its boxes reach the chat), or thinner than the text allows. Out it is: the
height, the sizes and the stacking are the design's, the bar still lies over the outer
diamond, and its outer end (498) is where the game's own frames had their middle.

**Choice: the cast bar is 12 tall, not hair-thin,** so the spell's name fits in it (10
units of text). That is the room between the largest diamonds (124.8) and the bar (141).

**Choice: the tooltip's corner is BOTTOMRIGHT -10, 10.** The tooltip grows up and left
from that corner (`ControllerLayout.lua`, TOOLTIP). A tooltip taller than 117 reaches up
beside the target bar's last 25 units; the check takes it at 200 by 100 as 0.16.0 does. A
tall one over the bar's end is accepted (0.16.0 accepted the same against the quest list).

While a sub-menu wheel is open (175 across, centred 138 either side, 188 up) its rim
reaches 7 units into the bars' inner ends. Accepted, as for the spell pictures.

The game's four frames stay where 0.16.x puts them (Edit Mode's spots), invisible. The
chat and the PE button keep the spots that were worked out around those frames; moving
them into the freed room is not part of this release.

`Plan.Rects(opts)` with `slim = 1` lists the seven slim rectangles and the tooltip's new
spot, and leaves out the six rectangles of the game's invisible frames (player, pet,
target, target of target, target auras, target cast bar). `Plan.SlimRects()` gives the
seven by name; `SlimBars.lua` places from it, so the test and the game use one set of
numbers.

## What each bar shows

Flat: a black see-through ground (0.6), a black line 1 unit wide around it, the game's
plain bar texture (`Interface\TargetingFrame\UI-StatusBar`, the one the game's own mana
bar uses, `U/Mainline/UnitFrame.lua:425`) in one colour. No portrait, no gold.

- **Player bar:** health (green) with the level and the name on its left and the health
  number on its right; under it the power bar in the power's colour (mana blue, rage red,
  focus orange, energy yellow: the game's own colours, `U/Mainline/PowerBarColorUtil.lua:18-21`).
- **Target bar:** the same for the target. Health red when the player can attack it, grey
  when another player's group tagged it, green otherwise.
- **Pet bar, target of target bar:** health and the name. No power, no number.
- **Cast bar:** the target's cast or channel filling left to right, the spell's name on it.

**Choice: a channel fills up like a cast** (the game's own bar empties for a channel). To
empty it the addon would have to subtract two times the game may hide; see "In a fight".

**Choice: the game's two settings "Show target of target" and "Show enemy cast bar" are
not read.** Both bars are pieces of this design; they show whenever there is something to
show.

Text sizes: 12 on the two main bars, 11 on the two small ones, 10 on the cast bar, the
game's standard font with an outline (as the spell pictures' counts).

Nothing is clicked on a bar: the bars do not take the mouse.

## When each bar shows, and what it listens to

| Bar | Shown while | Events |
|---|---|---|
| Player | always | `UNIT_HEALTH`, `UNIT_MAXHEALTH`, `UNIT_POWER_UPDATE`, `UNIT_MAXPOWER`, `UNIT_DISPLAYPOWER`, `UNIT_NAME_UPDATE`, `UNIT_LEVEL` for "player"; `PLAYER_LEVEL_UP` |
| Pet | `UnitExists("pet")` | the health and name events for "pet"; `UNIT_PET` for "player" |
| Target | `UnitExists("target")` | the player's list for "target"; `UNIT_FACTION`; `PLAYER_TARGET_CHANGED` |
| Target of target | the target exists and `UnitExists("targettarget")` | `UNIT_TARGET` for "target"; `PLAYER_TARGET_CHANGED` |
| Cast | the target is casting or channelling | `UNIT_SPELLCAST_START`, `_STOP`, `_FAILED`, `_INTERRUPTED`, `_DELAYED`, `_CHANNEL_START`, `_CHANNEL_UPDATE`, `_CHANNEL_STOP` for "target"; `PLAYER_TARGET_CHANGED` |
| All | | `PLAYER_ENTERING_WORLD` (everything read again) |

The unit events are registered with `RegisterUnitEvent` (`DOC/SimpleFrameAPIDocumentation.lua:1064`,
the call the game's own frames use, `U/Mainline/UnitFrame.lua:90-125`, `:856`), on two
frames of ours: one for "player" and "pet", one for "target". They are the events the
game's own frames use (`U/Mainline/UnitFrame.lua:82-125, 856-877`,
`U/Mainline/TargetFrame.lua:165-215`, `U/Mainline/PetFrame.lua:42-48`,
`Blizzard_UIPanels_Game/Shared/CastingBarFrame.lua:238-250`); all are in
`DOC/UnitDocumentation.lua` (3832-4871).

No timer. One `OnUpdate`, on the cast bar's own frame, set when a cast shows and taken off
when it hides: each frame it gives the bar the time, and twice a second it asks the game
whether the cast is still going (so a missed "stopped" event cannot leave a full bar
behind).

**Choice: the target of target's health follows the other bars' events.** The game sends
no health event for "targettarget"; its own frame reads it every frame
(`U/Mainline/TargetFrame.lua:887-890, 907`). Ours is read again whenever the target or
its target changes and whenever a health event arrives for the player, the pet or the
target. When the target's target is the player or the pet (the usual case) that is
exact; for anyone else it can lag until the next of those events. A slow tick while the
bar shows would close the gap; it is left out because nothing may tick, and the device
test asks whether the lag is seen.

## In a fight

The game hides some values from addons in a fight. The documentation says which:
`UnitHealth` always may (`DOC/UnitDocumentation.lua:1526`, SecretReturns), `UnitHealthMax`
(`:1472`), `UnitPower` (`:2732`), `UnitPowerMax` (`:2801`), `UnitName` (`:2489`) and every
answer of `UnitCastingInfo` / `UnitChannelInfo` but three (`:828-852`, `:870-894`) when
their restriction is on. And which widget calls take such a value from an addon
(`SecretArguments = "AllowedWhenTainted"`): `StatusBar:SetMinMaxValues` and `SetValue`
(`DOC/SimpleStatusBarAPIDocumentation.lua:229, 355`) and `FontString:SetText`
(`DOC/SimpleFontStringAPIDocumentation.lua:664`).

So every such value goes from the game's function straight into one of those three calls
and is never compared, added, joined to text, formatted or tested:

- health: `SetMinMaxValues(0, UnitHealthMax(unit))`, `SetValue(UnitHealth(unit))`, and the
  number as `SetText(UnitHealth(unit))`;
- power: the same with `UnitPowerMax` and `UnitPower`;
- name: `SetText(UnitName(unit))`;
- cast: `SetMinMaxValues(startTimeMs, endTimeMs)` with the two times as the game gave
  them, and `SetValue(GetTime() * 1000)`, our own clock, which is not hidden. The game's
  bar does the subtraction itself (`CastingBarFrame.lua:396-401`); ours leaves it to the
  widget. The name as `SetText(displayName)`.

What decides something is only read from answers the documentation does not mark as
hidden: `UnitExists` (`:1192`), `UnitLevel` (`:2474`), `UnitCanAttack` (`:763`),
`UnitIsTapDenied` (`:2365`), `UnitPowerType` (`:2859`), and for "is it casting" the sixth
answer of the two cast functions, `isTradeskill`, marked NeverSecret (`:845`, `:887`): it
is there exactly while a cast is. Should one of these come back hidden after all, it is
not tested either: the bar keeps its last state (or the default colour) and the debug log
says once which call it was.

**Choice: `StatusBar:SetTimerDuration` with `UnitCastingDuration` is not used** for the
cast bar, though it would need no `OnUpdate`: `SetTimerDuration` is documented as taking
hidden values from the game's own code only (`DOC/SimpleStatusBarAPIDocumentation.lua:334-336`)
and `UnitCastingDuration` as answering with one (`DOC/UnitDocumentation.lua:811-815`).

Nothing of the game's is changed in a fight. Showing, hiding and filling our own plain
frames is allowed in a fight (the spell pictures do it, device-proven). The game's frames'
see-through is only set out of a fight.

## The game's four frames: invisible, alive

How: `SetAlpha(0)` on `PlayerFrame`, `PetFrame`, `TargetFrame` and `TargetFrame.totFrame`,
each one's see-through read first with `GetAlpha` and put back with `SetAlpha` when the
bars stop. Nothing else of theirs is called and no field is written.

Why that call is safe by the source:

- The unit frames are Edit Mode systems (`U/Mainline/PlayerFrame.xml:14`,
  `PetFrame.xml:11`, `TargetFrame.xml:549`), and Edit Mode replaces five methods on a
  system frame with Lua: `SetScale`, `SetPoint`, `ClearAllPoints`, `SetShown`, `Hide`
  (`E/Shared/EditModeSystemTemplates.lua:11-18, 33-36`). `SetAlpha` and `GetAlpha` are not
  among them, and no mixin of these frames defines one (the only Lua `SetAlpha` functions
  in the whole source are `HANDLE`, `ItemButtonMixin` and `PowerDependencyLineMixin`). So
  it is the engine's own method, as on the bar anchors, where it is device-proven.
- The engine's `SetAlpha` is not a protected call in the documentation
  (`DOC/SimpleFrameAPIDocumentation.lua:1134`); it is still only made out of a fight.
- The game itself never sets these frames' see-through: the Edit Mode setting "Opacity"
  (`EditModeSystemTemplates.lua:1586-1591`) is only applied where a layout has it
  (`:1654`, `:1691-1692`), and the presets give it to the party, raid and arena frames
  only (`E/Mainline/EditModePresetLayouts.lua:303, 328, 365`; the player, target and pet
  entries have none, `:249-275, 379-383`). No other code in `Blizzard_UnitFrame` sets a
  whole frame's see-through.
- No part of the three frames ignores its parent's see-through (`ignoreParentAlpha`
  appears in `U/` only in the arena and party templates).
- The pet frame is made as the player frame's child (`PetFrame.xml:11`) but Edit Mode
  moves it to `UIParent` once it has a spot of its own (`EditModeSystemTemplates.lua:335-343,
  350-365`), which our layout gives it. So it gets its own call. The target of target
  (`U/Mainline/TargetFrame.lua:732-737`) and the game's target cast bar (`:705-715`) are
  the target frame's children and go with it; the target of target still gets its own call
  so each of the four is handled the same way.

Order: our bars are built and shown first, the game's frames made invisible after, so a
bar that cannot be built leaves the game's frames showing. The game's frames are shown
again before our bars are hidden.

Unproven until the device: that the see-through stays at 0 (through fights, a new
target, a vehicle, a zone change). Each time the world loads, and each time the layout is
placed again, the four are read and set to 0 again if needed, out of a fight, with a line
in the debug log. The snapshot lists the four frames with their see-through.

### A tap on an invisible frame

By the source the frames still take the mouse at see-through 0: each is a
`SecureUnitButtonTemplate` (`PlayerFrame.xml:14`, `PetFrame.xml:11`, `TargetFrame.xml:52,
397`), set up so a left click targets the unit and a right click opens its menu
(`Blizzard_FrameXML/SecureTemplates.lua:828-839`; `U/Mainline/PlayerFrame.lua:100`,
`PetFrame.lua:70`, `TargetFrame.lua:115, 747`), and a pointer over it opens its tooltip.
Left alone, a tap on what looks like empty ground at the bottom left would target
yourself (in a fight: lose your enemy) or open a menu from nowhere. Not acceptable.

**Choice: a tap guard over each of the four,** as under the spell pictures: a plain
frame of ours that takes the mouse and does nothing, one layer above the game's frame
(the game's are LOW, ours MEDIUM), placed out of a fight on the rectangle read from the
game's frame, shown while that frame's bar shows. A tap there does nothing. The game's
frame is not changed. The cost: the menus those frames open by right click cannot be
reached while Slim bars is On, and those two patches of ground (232 by 100 each, bottom
left and right, where the frames were anyway) still do not pass a tap to the world. If a
frame's rectangle cannot be read, that guard is left out and the log says so.

## The switch

"Slim bars", On / Off, default Off, on the Controller tab, saved with the layout's
options (`opts.slim`, 0 or 1).

**It counts from the next reload**, like the part switches, and the reload is asked for
at once: the tooltip's spot is a piece of the Edit Mode layout, which only changes with a
rewrite and a reload (as the Spell panel size does). The value the session started with
decides whether `SlimBars.lua` runs. A change in a fight waits for the fight to end before
the layout is rewritten (`PE:RunOutOfCombat`). Refused while Edit Mode is open, like the
other layout rows.

Off means the file does nothing: no frame made, no event registered, no hook (there is
none at all), no game call; the game's frames are as 0.16.4 leaves them and the tooltip is
where 0.16.4 puts it.

The Controller layout going off, or another Edit Mode layout being picked, stops the
bars (out of a fight): the game's four frames get their see-through back, ours hide, every
event is dropped. If that happens in a fight, the bars stay as they are until the fight
ends, then the same.

If the bars hit an error three times in a session they turn themselves off: ours hide at
once, the game's frames come back (after the fight, if one is on), chat says so, and the
card's status line says why until a reload.

## Logging

With the Debug log on, key `ctrlLayout`, each line starting "slim bars:":

- on start: one line with the switch's value and the Spell panel size; one line per bar
  with its anchor, x, y, width, height and scale; one line per game frame made invisible
  (with the see-through it had) or not found; one per tap guard placed or left out;
- on stop: one line, with why (the layout went off, an error), and one per game frame put
  back;
- each time a bar is shown or hidden, with the reason ("no pet", "no target", "the target
  has no target", "the target is casting", "the cast ended", "interrupted", ...);
- each game function that is missing or raises, by name, once;
- each time the game's frames had to be made invisible again.

Never a line for a health, power or name update.

The snapshot (`PE.ControllerLayout:AddSnapshotSource`) lists the five bars and the four
tap guards; the game's four frames are already in it.

## Tests (written first)

`tests/features/slimbars_test.lua`, `layout_test.lua`; the fake client gets a status bar,
unit events, the unit functions and the three unit frames, each with the source line it
stands for.

- the plan: the seven rectangles' numbers; with `slim = 1` no piece overlaps another at
  every Spell panel size, with the controls reminder on and off, the map buttons shown and
  hidden, and against the diamonds of each trigger state; the tooltip's piece in the
  corner; `slim = 0` leaves the plan exactly as 0.16.4 has it;
- the switch: Off by default, a saved 2 not kept; a change rewrites only the tooltip's
  piece and asks for the reload; in a fight it waits; with Edit Mode open it is put back;
- Off does nothing: no frame from the file, no event, no game call, the frames untouched;
- start: the bars at their numbers; the four frames at 0 after the bars show; never one of
  Edit Mode's five replaced methods on them and no field written;
- hidden values go into the widgets untouched (the very same value arrives) for health,
  power, name and the cast's two times, and the widgets refusing them is logged, not
  raised;
- no pet, a pet arriving and leaving; no target, a target, a target of target appearing
  and going;
- a cast starting, being pushed back, finishing, being interrupted, a channel, a target
  change in mid-cast, a missed stop event; the `OnUpdate` only while a cast shows;
- stop puts the four frames back as they were; the layout going off does; in a fight
  nothing of the game's is called until it ends;
- three errors turn the bars off.

Tests prove the logic. Only the device can show:

- that the game's frames really go invisible, stay so, and come back;
- that no "action blocked" follows from the see-through or the guards;
- that the widgets take the hidden values in a real fight (health, the health number, the
  target's name, the cast's times);
- that the cast bar's time and the game's are the same clock;
- how the numbers look on the 720p screen, and whether the target of target's health lags;
- what a class's own pieces (combo points, totems) do over an invisible player frame.

Each has its fallback in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`; the plainest one is the switch itself.

## As built

- **Files.** `addons/PocketEssentials/SlimBars.lua` (new, in the TOC after `FaceBars.lua`);
  `ControllerLayout.lua` gained `Plan.SLIM`, `Plan.SlimRects`, the `slim` option and its
  row, and `Snap.SlimCall` / `Snap.slimOn` (on the existing `Snap` table: the file has no
  room for a local). Tests: `tests/features/slimbars_test.lua` (34 tests) and a block at
  the end of `layout_test.lua` (12).
- **Names in the plan.** `Plan.SlimRects()` calls the seven "player bar", "target bar",
  "cast bar", "pet bar", "target of target bar", "pet boxes room" and "target boxes room".
  "target cast bar" stays the name of the game's own, which is left out of `Plan.Rects`
  while the switch is on.
- **The session's value.** `Snap.slimOn` is read from the option at the first placement of
  a session and never changes after. Off, `Snap.SlimCall` returns before it looks at
  `PE.SlimBars`: the file is not even asked for its status.
- **Start while running** (the layout is placed again at every world load and layout
  event) builds nothing and registers nothing: it reads the four frames' see-through,
  sets 0 again where the game showed one (with a log line), and places the tap guards
  again. The same check runs on `PLAYER_ENTERING_WORLD`.
- **Stop** is not called by the layout in a fight at all (the layout's put-back waits for
  the fight to end, and the bars with it); asked directly in a fight it waits too. This
  differs from the spell pictures, which stop at once: their bars' see-through is
  device-proven in a fight, the unit frames' is not, and a fight is the worst time to have
  no health display.
- **The safety net in a fight:** after three errors our bars hide at once, so no stale
  number shows, and the game's frames come back when the fight ends. Worst case: no
  health display for the rest of that one fight.
- **The public functions** are set by string key (`Slim["Stop"]`), as in `FaceBars.lua`:
  the API check lists widget methods by name and "Stop" is also an animation's.
- **The game's functions** are read once, at the first Start, into a table and called by
  name through one guarded helper, so a missing or failing one is named in the log once.
- **Colours.** Friend 0.10, 0.65, 0.20; enemy 0.80, 0.15, 0.15; tagged 0.50 grey; cast
  0.90, 0.70, 0.10; mana 0.20, 0.40, 1.00 (the game's 0, 0, 1 is too dark on the ground);
  rage, focus and energy the game's.
- **The border** is 1 UI unit, a little under one screen pixel at 720p: whether it draws
  as a clean line is for the device.
- **Edit Mode.** While Slim bars is On the game's four frames are invisible in Edit Mode
  too, so they cannot be seen there to be moved; nothing depends on where they are but
  the tap guards, which follow them at the next placement.
- **The fake client** got status bars, `RegisterUnitEvent`, the unit functions, secret
  values that raise when used, and (only where a test asks, `c:addUnitFrames()`) the player
  and pet frames with all three unit frames guarded: a call to one of Edit Mode's five
  replaced methods, a key written, or `SetAlpha` in a fight fails the test. The probe's
  tests keep the client without `RegisterUnitEvent` they were written for.
- **The taint check** has a line for each new name; no call that writes was added. The
  one line worth reading is `SetAlpha` in `SlimBars.lua`.
- **Not done:** the buff and debuff boxes (waiting for the probe); a slow tick for the
  target of target's health; the game's "Show target of target" and "Show enemy cast bar"
  settings; moving the chat and the PE button into the room the game's frames left.
